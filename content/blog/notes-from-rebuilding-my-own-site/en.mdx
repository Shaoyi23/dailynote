I rebuilt my site for the same reason I reorganize a desk: not because it was broken, but because I wanted to understand what I actually used. A personal site makes that question unusually honest. No roadmap, no stakeholder, no one asking for another section.

## start with the reading column

The first decision was width. A narrower column makes almost every other choice easier. Type gets calmer, line breaks get more intentional, and decoration has less room to become the point.

The second decision was what not to include. No analytics, no newsletter popup, no giant case study carousel. A short introduction, a few links, and a way to reach me. Restraint is easier to describe than to ship.

- Write the copy before choosing the layout.
- Use one accent per idea, not one per section.
- If a detail is only there to impress, remove it.

The site will change again. That is part of the point. For now it does the small thing I wanted it to do: it gets out of the way and lets the writing be the interface.
