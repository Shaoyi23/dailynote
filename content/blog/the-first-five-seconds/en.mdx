Most product pages ask for attention before they have earned it. A logo animates in, a gradient breathes, a headline waits for its moment. None of it matters if the page has not told me what it is and what I can do next.

## keep the first screen honest

The opening screen is not a poster. It is a conversation. The fastest interfaces make the first move small: one clear sentence, one obvious action, and enough visual rhythm that my eye knows where to land without being pushed.

> Clarity is a form of speed.

I keep a short checklist when a screen feels louder than it needs to be. Usually the answer is not another feature. It is removing the thing that made me explain the page in the first place.

- Say what the product is in plain language.
- Make the primary action visible before it is clever.
- Let motion explain a change, not decorate a silence.
