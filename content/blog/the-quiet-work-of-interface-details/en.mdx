The parts of an interface I remember are rarely the big ones. A button that settles instead of snapping. An empty state that says something true. A focus ring that appears exactly when it should.

## details are decisions

It is tempting to treat detail work as a final pass, something to do after the real product is finished. But the line height, the delay before a tooltip, and the wording of an error message are all product decisions. They just happen to live in the seams.

When those seams are ignored, people do not usually complain. They just feel slightly less certain. The cost is hard to measure, which is exactly why it is easy to skip.

> Good detail work is mostly the removal of tiny moments of doubt.

I try to leave a little room in every build for this kind of work. Not because it photographs well, but because it is the difference between a product that works and one that feels considered.
